02
Heart Failure Mortality Prediction
Clinical ML
Case study +
The stakes

299 real patients. 13 clinical features. 30-day mortality. Heart failure kills and which features predict it matters more than which model wins. I refused to stop at accuracy. I wanted to know what the model was actually seeing.

The discovery

Benchmarked 4 classifiers (LR, Decision Tree, Random Forest, SVM). But the real finding: serum creatinine + ejection fraction are the two root predictors not the other 11 features. That's not a stat. That's a clinical insight. 85%+ accuracy. 12 annotated visualisations.

The process

End-to-end EDA before any modelling. Feature importance after. A fully reproducible Jupyter pipeline not just a notebook with outputs, but documented decisions at every step so someone else could pick it up and understand the reasoning, not just the results.

03
Neural Network - NumPy Only
From Scratch
Case study +
Why I chose to suffer

Everyone uses Keras. Nobody understands what Keras does. Calling model.fit() and celebrating accuracy felt dishonest. I needed to implement every operation by hand, feel the chain rule, break the gradient flow, fix the numerical edge cases before I'd trust myself to use any framework.

What nobody noticed I added

The standard implementation: Xavier init, ReLU, softmax, cross-entropy, mini-batch backprop. What I added: overflow-safe softmax and log smoothing in cross entropy production-level safeguards no tutorial mentions, because I'd read about where the numbers silently explode.

The proof

96%+ accuracy on 70,000 MNIST images. Zero TensorFlow. Zero Keras. Zero PyTorch. Every gradient computed by hand. Batch size 64, learning rate 0.01, hidden layer 128 neurons all tuned by understanding the math, not by grid search.

Customer Churn Prediction
Industry · Inspire Softech
Case study +
The real question

Real company. Real 10,000-user Spotify dataset. Predicting who churns is the easy part every model can do that. Understanding why they churn, well enough for a retention team to actually act on it, is the hard part. I refused to stop at accuracy.

What I delivered

XGBoost: F1 = 0.87, ROC-AUC = 0.91. Then used SHAP to rank the top 10 predictive features not just model accuracy, but explainability. K-Means segmented users into 3 behavioural clusters. CLV metric surfaced the top 5 high value accounts for a retention campaign.

The thinking

Also built a binary purchase propensity model from raw subscription and playlist data Random Forest reaching 89% accuracy at flagging premium upgrade candidates. A Plotly 3D visualisation defined the personalised retention strategies for each cluster.

Sales Demand Forecasting
Time Series · Feb 2026
Case study +
The approach

Most forecasting projects pick one model and declare victory. Real data science teams compare models, track experiments, document findings. I built this project the way it would be built at a company with rigour, not just results.

What I found

3 years of retail data. 1,000 real transactions (2012–2015). ARIMA vs Prophet compared on MAPE, every run tracked in MLflow. Discovery: Q4 spikes consistently every year. Technology = highest revenue category. Insights that a business could act on Monday morning.

The reproducibility principle

All findings saved as charts. Anyone can reproduce every result with a single command. That's not a bonus feature, it's a baseline for how data science should work. MLflow logs every parameter, every metric, every artefact. No black boxes.

06
VERITAS — AI Fact Verification
Multi Agent AI
Case study +
The root cause

Misinformation spreads at machine speed. Fact checkers work at human speed. That's not a content problem it's a speed asymmetry problem. I asked: what's the actual bottleneck? Verification latency. So I built something to close that gap.

What VERITAS does

Multiple AI agents working in parallel + real time web search to verify any text claim or image. Built on n8n. Designed from first principles: not "can AI help with fact checking?" but "what architecture lets verification happen at the speed misinformation travels?"

Why it matters

Slowing down to fact check isn't an option anymore. The system is designed to be as fast as a share button because that's the only speed that matters when misinformation is already halfway around the world before truth has its boots on.
